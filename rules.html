The Rules of Pool

The most common way to play is 8-ball. Below are the standard rules, plus a quick guide to 9-ball. House rules vary, so agree on any differences before you break.

Setting up: the rack

Pool is played with a cue ball and 15 numbered object balls. Balls 1–7 are solid colours ("solids"), 8 is black, and 9–15 are striped ("stripes"). To rack for 8-ball:

  • Place the balls in the triangle rack at the foot of the table.
  • The 8-ball goes in the centre of the third row.
  • A solid and a stripe go in the two back corners.
  • The apex ball sits on the foot spot; the rest are placed randomly.

The break

  1. Place the cue ball anywhere behind the head string (the "kitchen").
  2. Strike the lead ball firmly. A legal break must either pot a ball or drive at least four object balls to the rails.
  3. If you pot a ball on the break, you continue your turn. The table stays "open" until a legal shot decides solids or stripes.
  4. If the 8-ball is potted on the break, you may re-rack and break again (common house rule) or spot the 8-ball.
Open table Right after the break the table is "open" — you can aim at a solid or a stripe. The group you legally pot first (on a called shot) becomes yours for the rest of the rack.

Taking your turn

You keep shooting as long as you legally pot one of your own balls on each shot. Your turn ends when you fail to pot one of your balls, or commit a foul.

Winning the game

Pot all of your group (solids or stripes), then legally pot the 8-ball in a called pocket. You must call which pocket the 8-ball is going into.

Fouls

Committing a foul ends your turn and usually gives your opponent ball in hand (they may place the cue ball anywhere on the table). Common fouls include:

FoulWhat happened
ScratchThe cue ball is potted or knocked off the table.
Wrong ball firstThe cue ball hits an opponent's ball (or the 8-ball) before one of yours.
No railAfter contact, no ball is potted and no ball reaches a cushion.
Off the tableAny ball is driven off the playing surface.
Double hit / pushThe cue tip contacts the cue ball more than once.
Losing the game (instant loss) You lose immediately if you pot the 8-ball early, pot the 8-ball on the same shot as your last group ball into the wrong pocket, or pot the 8-ball in an uncalled pocket / knock it off the table.

9-ball in brief

9-ball is a faster, lower-scoring game played with balls 1 through 9.

  • Rack the nine balls in a diamond shape with the 1-ball at the apex and the 9-ball in the centre.
  • You must always strike the lowest-numbered ball first, but any ball potted on a legal shot counts.
  • The player who legally pots the 9-ball wins the rack — this can happen early via a combination.
