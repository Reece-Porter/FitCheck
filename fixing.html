Fixing & Maintaining a Pool Table

A well-kept table plays true for decades. Here are the most common fixes and upkeep tasks, from a five-minute brush to a weekend re-felt.

Before you start Work with the table cleared and, for bigger jobs, have a helper. Slate is extremely heavy — never try to move a slate table on your own.

Tools you may need

  • Spirit level (or carpenter's level)
  • Table brush
  • Shims / levelling feet
  • Staple gun or spray adhesive
  • Cue tip & glue
  • Fine sandpaper
  • Screwdriver & socket set
  • Beeswax / seam filler

1. Levelling the table

A level bed is the single most important thing. If balls drift to one side, the table is out of level.

  1. Place a spirit level on the slate (or the cloth) in the centre, then check it lengthwise, widthwise, and diagonally.
  2. If the table has adjustable feet, turn them up or down to raise or lower each corner until the bubble is centred.
  3. For non-adjustable tables, slide thin shims under the low legs.
  4. Re-check every position — adjusting one corner affects the others. Repeat until level from all directions.
Watch the floor Even a level table drifts if the floor settles. Re-check the level a couple of times a year, and after moving the table.

2. Cleaning & brushing the felt

Chalk dust and grime slow the cloth and cause miscues.

  • Brush after every session: short, straight strokes from the head to the foot (one direction), never scrubbing in circles.
  • Vacuum occasionally on low suction with a soft brush attachment to lift embedded dust.
  • Wipe balls with a damp cloth and dry them — dirty balls grind grit into the cloth.
  • Keep drinks off the table and use a fitted cover when it is not in use.

3. Fixing loose or dead cushions

If balls don't rebound properly, the rubber cushions may be worn, dried out, or coming loose from the rail.

  1. Unbolt the rails from underneath and lift them off.
  2. Check the rubber: if it is hard, cracked, or crumbling, the cushions need replacing.
  3. Peel the cloth and old rubber off the rail; scrape away old glue.
  4. Glue new K-66 profile cushion rubber to the rail, trim the ends flush, then re-cover with cloth and re-attach the rails.

4. Repairing or replacing the felt

Small nicks can be lived with, but a torn, worn, or badly stained cloth should be replaced.

  1. Remove the rails and detach the old cloth (it is stapled or glued to the frame).
  2. Clean the slate; fill any seams or screw holes with beeswax and scrape smooth.
  3. Lay the new cloth over the slate, smoothing out every wrinkle from the centre outward.
  4. Stretch it taut and staple (or glue) it to the underside of the frame, working from the centre of each side toward the corners.
  5. Cut pocket openings carefully and re-fit the rails and pocket liners.
Not sure? Re-felting and re-cushioning take patience and an even hand. If the table is valuable or antique, a professional recover is money well spent.

5. Re-tipping a cue

A worn, flattened, or loose tip causes miscues. Replacing it is a quick fix.

  1. Peel or cut off the old tip and sand the ferrule flat.
  2. Apply cue-tip glue and press a new tip firmly into place; let it cure.
  3. Trim the tip flush with the ferrule and sand the edges to match.
  4. Shape the tip to a slight dome (a coin's curve) and scuff it so it holds chalk.
